# Hidden instruction component corpus

D02 T05 §7. Generated offline on 4 November 2026 from project-owned synthetic fixtures and deterministic variants. No client mail, external dataset, model calls, accounts or paid service is used. All new text is synthetic project-owned evaluation data. Sources: `test/checks/sparse-invisible.test.ts`, `test/checks/document-text.test.ts`, `test/fixtures/attachments/archives.ts` and `test/fixtures/attachments/pdfs.ts`. Each case records its source and SHA-256 of the stored UTF-8 payload field. Document payloads are base64; their case hash covers that base64 string, not decoded bytes.

Run `taskset -c 3 node scripts/eval-hidden-text.ts`. The runner generates and validates the corpus, calls the production checks, and writes `corpus.json`, `observations.json` and `results.md` atomically per file. `--corpus FILE --out DIRECTORY` measures a supplied corpus without modifying its input file. Exit 0 means all targets passed, 1 means a completed measurement missed a target, and 2 means invalid input, a reader failure or an operational failure. A failed input validation publishes no new result; an older result retains its original date and digest and must not be mistaken for the failed attempt. Reader failures retain every case with explicit error status. Individual files are atomic, not a multi-file transaction; compare the report and observation corpus digest before consuming them.

The 410 cases contain 205 attacks and 205 controls. Each label includes 25 cases in each of English, Afrikaans, isiZulu, French, Spanish, German, Hindi and Persian, plus five English PDF cases. Five primary techniques each have five variants per language and label: sparse default-ignorable characters, Unicode tag payloads, folded Base64 subjects, folded quoted-printable subjects and DOCX text split across XML runs. PDF cases reuse WinAnsi, split-TJ and plain fixtures at five stream chunk sizes. The DOCX reader is tested on a minimal synthetic archive with a valid member checksum, not a full Office rendering benchmark.

Eight language labels describe surrounding business text, not eight validated detector languages. Most steering instructions remain English; variant four outside the ASCII-only tag technique uses a translated steering probe. Translations are synthetic, not independently linguist-reviewed. Tag characters encode ASCII and cannot honestly carry all eight scripts. Chunk sizes are 1, 7, 31, 4096 and 13 UTF-16 code units, including supplementary-character splits. Controls exercise joiners, selectors, family emoji, a keycap and an RGI subdivision flag. Templates, payloads and PDF fixture reuse are correlated; the case count must not be presented as independent real-world samples or an accuracy confidence interval.

A flag is at least one production instruction match (high), or an invisible run of medium or high severity. Low-only ignorable runs are retained in observations but do not count as flags. A missed attack has no such flag or failed detection. A false flag is a successfully measured control with such a flag. Control reader failures remain errors, retain their denominator, have null flag status, and force overall failure; they are never clean controls. All findings retain their patterns, locations and severities. This is evidence-level detection, not a verdict measurement.

Targets were committed before execution: miss rate at most 5%, false-flag rate at most 2%, p50 at most 10 ms and p95 at most 50 ms per case. Timing includes subject decoding or document extraction and production streaming detection; it excludes imports, corpus generation and disk I/O. One ordered pass has no warmup; nearest-rank percentiles include failed attempts. Node version, platform, source commit, source dirty state and corpus digest accompany each run. Local timings are not Worker or forward-to-reply timings. Misses remain in the report and are escalated to the coordinator; this lane does not alter production detection or adjust labels to make targets pass.
