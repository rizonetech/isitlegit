**Part 2: excellence**

**Comparator: Bitdefender Scamio**, a strong direct alternative for getting a first assessment of suspicious mail. Its documented web flow is account creation/sign-in, submission of text/link/image, then results and advice. This establishes the workflow, not independently measured latency, accessibility or recovery quality. [Bitdefender’s product documentation](https://www.bitdefender.com/en-au/consumer/scamio)

1. **out-of-scope: the guided first-check path introduces avoidable navigation and context switching.** From this checklist, the prescribed route takes one click to forwarding instructions, then a mail-client switch, attachment forwarding and a return to results. Scamio documents submission directly after sign-in. The existing shell’s “Check a file” helps, but the checklist does not explain that checking can precede domain/team setup. **Target:** after sign-in, at most one navigation to submission, no domain/team prerequisite, and an explicit route to the resulting report. Changing the approved checklist treatment needs a **07-app design/section decision**, retaining attachment forwarding and the existing upload route.

2. **in-scope: the claimed speed advantage has no measured evidence.** “Most teams finish in about ten minutes” is displayed, but these tests measure neither setup completion nor first-verdict time. Scamio’s “seconds” claim is also marketing evidence, not a benchmark. **Target:** measure identical first-check tasks on a representative phone/mobile connection; meet the project’s median forward-to-reply under 60 seconds and p95 under three minutes, and demonstrate lower median onboarding-to-verdict time than the comparator before claiming superiority.

3. **in-scope: completion feedback fails the result-first standard.** The premature report claim and overstated supplier/protection claims identified above prevent users from distinguishing setup activity from a usable result. **Target:** zero completed-report claims for pending/failed cases and zero protection claims unsupported by current state; cover each with a regression test. This fits the existing surface and requires no weaker safety behaviour.

I found no substantiated competitor-relative accessibility, crash-recovery or large-file performance shortfall to add. This candidate itself does not process files.
