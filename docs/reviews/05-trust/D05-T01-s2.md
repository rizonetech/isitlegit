1. **P1, lines 99-100: The matcher cannot serve the planned counterparty consumer.** `callerOrgId` is mandatory, but D05 T02 §1 accepts senders without an organisation and assigns the case to the protected business. Add a trusted caller identity supporting both members and non-members, with per-sender and per-business limits. Test two non-member senders against one business without inventing an organisation or using the target as the caller.

2. **P1, line 99: No registered accounts becomes false bank-change evidence.** §1 permits effective protection without an account, and removal can leave zero accounts. Returning `{ match: false }` then triggers critical findings in both consumers despite there being no registered baseline. Add a typed unavailable-baseline refusal, distinct from mismatch, and tests before the first account and after removing the last.

3. **P1, lines 85, 95-96, 99, 102: Changing the HMAC key silently turns genuine accounts into mismatches.** The recorded ciphertext key fingerprint and decrypt test cover the encryption key, but matching has no persisted HMAC-key compatibility check. Require identifiers for both keys and refuse matching or adding into an incompatible registry. Test replacement of each key independently: neither may produce a false mismatch or bypass duplicate detection.

4. **P1, lines 97, 99, 106: Match-input validation and canonicalisation are unspecified and unproven.** Registration removes spaces and dashes, but matching does not require the same transformation or typed refusals for malformed number and branch inputs. Require shared strict validation, preservation of leading zeroes, and tests matching formatted and canonical representations. Prove a different number and, separately, a different branch do not match. The current generic “match and no match” test can pass while branch comparison is absent.

5. **P1, lines 97, 106: The encryption proof passes a plaintext implementation.** A round trip through `decryptForOwner` does not prove encryption at rest, AES-GCM authentication or fresh IVs. Add assertions against persisted rows, independent Web Crypto decryption, distinct 96-bit IVs across writes, and refusal after ciphertext or authentication-tag corruption. The checkpoint must fail when encryption is replaced by plaintext storage, even if public return values remain masked.

6. **P2, lines 98, 101, 106: Removal and console read-back are insufficiently tested.** “Removes an account” does not explicitly require the account to disappear from `maskedAccounts` and cease matching while another account remains. Define missing-account and repeated-removal behaviour, with no fabricated removal event. Assert the complete masked record, including ID, bank, branch, holder and mask: line 101’s negative-only assertions pass an implementation returning an empty list.

7. **P2, lines 97-100, 103: Atomicity and audit correctness lack failure-path proof.** Require injected audit-insert failures to roll back adds and removals, and concurrent matches to admit at most the configured limit. Specify how the two limit buckets and match audit commit together, including refused attempts and database failure, so failures cannot return unaudited success or consume only one bucket accidentally. Sequential 21st-call tests and merely finding events do not prove these properties.

8. **P2, line 103: The audit leak test rejects legitimate metadata and misses other leaks.** Grepping whole events for runs exceeding three digits rejects their year and potentially their IDs, while missing formatted account numbers and key material. Specify an allowlisted audit payload and assert exact actor, kind, result and event count. Test synthetic sensitive values in payloads, errors and captured logs, including formatted numbers and both keys.

9. **P2, lines 85, 102: Re-encryption is named but no recovery operation exists.** `decryptForOwner` alone cannot replace ciphertext, HMAC and key identifiers safely. Add an operator recovery procedure or helper covering retained old keys, atomic replacement, interrupted retries and permanent key loss, with round-trip and rollback tests. Lost-key records must remain explicitly unavailable until recovery or re-registration, never become mismatches.
