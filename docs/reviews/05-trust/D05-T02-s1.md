The tier-cap implementation is explicitly absent and its dependency remains “once named.” The resolver nevertheless reports no unmet dependencies. 2. **P1: Counterparty and member quotas interact without a defined contract** (lines 63 and 64). Counterparty cases belong to the protected organisation, but `admitCase` counts every organisation case against `org_limits.daily_cases`. External checks can therefore exhaust staff checks, and staff checks can prevent counterparty admission before the promised 50-business limit. Specify whether these budgets are independent, implement that decision in admission, and test both directions with mixed member and counterparty traffic.

3. **P1: The gate prevents later claim routes from working for non-members** (lines 48 and 63). Exact display-name/domain matching drops mail whose only protected-business claim is its logo, signature, voice attachment or copied badge. Sections §2 and §8 detect those claims only after admission; neither owns reopening this gate. Add an explicit admission path and tests for those supported claims, or name a section that extends the gate before those features ship.

4. **P2: Counter accounting lacks retry and failure semantics** (lines 52 and 64). One atomic upsert per bucket does not define how both buckets interact with deduplication, a refused second bucket, failed R2 storage or failed Workflow creation. Existing `admitDelivery` undoes failed admissions and supports redelivery. Require counter reservations to follow that lifecycle so retries do not consume additional checks and rejected admissions do not exhaust another bucket. Test duplicate delivery, rollback, concurrent last-slot admission, both limits and SAST rollover.

5. **P2: “One reply” lacks delivery-recovery proof** (line 65). `isAllowedRecipient` currently receives neither case ID nor send key, and `sendMail` checks it before delivery deduplication. A permission consumed on the first allowlist check could prevent retry after a failed send. Require case-bound authorization through the actual sending path, preserved for retries of the same logical reply and refused for unrelated sends. Test provider failure followed by recovery, duplicate execution and separate legitimate cases from the same sender.

6. **P2: Intake acceptance tests leave explicit requirements unproven** (lines 52, 63 and 69). “Claim, no claim and member mail” can pass without testing authenticated versus unauthenticated forwarders, ambiguous identities, inline forwards, Outlook `.msg` originals or persisted `origin` read-back. `extractOriginal` returns bytes and a format, not parsed From fields. Require fixtures exercising the real extraction and parsing paths, assertions that refused mail creates no case or Workflow, and database read-back proving the marker and protected organisation survive admission.

**Part 1: completeness**

1. **present:** Attached-original intake and business selection. `.eml` and `.msg` originals supply the claimed name/domain; exactly one protected organisation receives the case. 2. **missing-in-scope:** Authenticate the reply mailbox, not merely its domain. The new gate calls `senderAuthenticated(..., "domain")`. That accepts a passing DKIM signature from the same registrable domain without checking that the signed From equals the envelope sender. An attacker can replay their own provider-signed forward with another mailbox at that provider as MAIL FROM: the victim becomes `forwardedBy`, receives the reply and loses quota. The existing address-authentication branch already distinguishes these mailboxes. Evidence: [email.ts:143](../../../src/intake/email.ts#L143), [sender.ts:44](../../../src/intake/sender.ts#L44). Missing regression: a valid shared-provider signature with a different signed From must create no case, Workflow or reply.

Admission atomically counts five cases per sender and fifty per protected organisation per SAST day, excluding counterparty traffic from staff capacity. No unread settings are introduced. Evidence: `src/db/cases.ts:210`; limit, rollover, independent-budget and concurrency tests at `test/trust/counterparty-intake.test.ts:269`, `:293`, `:313`, `:368`.

4. **present:** Defined refusal paths. Oversized messages are rejected; automatic messages, unauthenticated forwards, inline forwards, unmatched claims, ambiguous claims and exhausted limits produce no counterparty case. Evidence: `src/intake/email.ts:77`, `:143`, `:167`, `:183`; tests at `test/intake/email.test.ts:266`, `:333` and `test/trust/counterparty-intake.test.ts:162`, `:195`. **Part 2: excellence**

**Comparator: Norton Genie**, a strong direct alternative for a recipient asking whether a suspicious email is a scam. Norton documents screenshot/text submission, explanations and next-step guidance, including when it cannot decide. It advertises results in seconds. These are vendor-documented behaviours, not independently measured timings or proof of registry verification. [Norton Genie FAQ](https://support.norton.com/sp/en/au~/solutions/v20230717145233467)

1. **out-of-scope:** Mobile submission is weaker. Genie accepts a screenshot or pasted text; this path requires an attached original and drops inline forwarding. The repository itself states that Gmail’s phone app cannot forward as attachment (`src/mail/reply.ts:188`). Desktop IsItLegit requires three conceptual actions: select attachment forwarding, address it, send. Genie requires opening its interface, supplying content and submitting; exact click counts were not measured. **Target:** a designed mobile counterparty path requiring no desktop transfer and at most three submission actions, while retaining mailbox proof, admission restrictions and honest evidence limitations. Owners: **D01 intake and D05 trust**, with design approval first.

2. **out-of-scope:** A mistaken submission offers no recovery guidance to its sender. Genie documents next-step advice when it cannot decide; an inline or malformed counterparty forward disappears into the same silence as an unauthorised probe. This follows §1’s deliberate refusal policy, so adding automatic refusal replies would weaken the decided boundary. **Target:** the public submission instructions explain attachment requirements, daily limits, expected response time and what to do after silence, with a comprehension test showing users can recover without operator help. Owner: **D05 T02 §6**, coordinated with **D07**.

3. **out-of-scope:** Speed superiority remains unproven. The gate parses the original before admission, and the Workflow parses it again (`src/intake/email.ts:179`, `src/pipeline/analysis-workflow.ts:354`). No counterparty latency comparison accompanies the candidate. This does not prove unacceptable performance, but it cannot support “better than Genie.” **Target:** measure the same synthetic corpus, including large and damaged supported messages; meet the existing median under 60 seconds and p95 under 180 seconds, then publish the measured comparison and any quality benefit that justifies extra time. Owner: **D08 eval**.

No comparative accessibility or crash-recovery superiority is claimed without equivalent measurements.
