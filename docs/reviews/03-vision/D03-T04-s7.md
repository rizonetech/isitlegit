Part 2: excellence

The strongest alternative for this consent boundary is a **local OCR plus named-entity pipeline using Microsoft Presidio, custom South African recognizers and the client dictionary, followed by independent verification**. Presidio supports text recognition and selective image-region redaction, providing a concrete comparison for privacy and cue preservation. This is an architectural comparator, not a measured winner on these emails; its image module also documents maturity limitations. [Presidio overview](https://microsoft.github.io/presidio/), [image-redactor documentation](https://github.com/data-privacy-stack/presidio/blob/main/docs/image-redactor/index.md).

1. **in-scope: recall within the promised detail types.** The demonstrated name, number-format and screenshot gaps prevent a credible claim of better privacy recall. **Proposed target:** zero surviving seeded details in accepted text, screenshots or records across a synthetic benchmark containing at least 1,000 entities, with results broken down by detail type and representation. Refusals must be reported separately from successful redaction.
2. **out-of-scope: discovering unlisted personal details.** The current dictionary/header approach cannot discover a person named only in a forwarded body or a street address omitted from the dictionary. Contextual entity recognition offers broader coverage. **Proposed target:** measure recall on deliberately unlisted people and addresses and match or exceed the configured comparator. Owner: **03-vision redaction expansion**; §7 presently specifies supplied names and header-derived names.
3. **out-of-scope: preserving visual lure cues.** Replacing every picture removes legitimate brand logos and other useful visual evidence, whereas selective redaction can preserve unaffected regions. This is an acknowledged privacy-first decision, not an unimplemented promise. **Proposed target:** preserve at least 95% of annotated nonpersonal brand/form cues while passing the same privacy benchmark. Owner: **03-vision OCR/redaction follow-up**, requiring revision of §7’s blanket-image policy.
4. **in-scope: operator acceptance and error recovery.** The operator must combine import status, inventory, a separate leak check and rebuild behavior, yet those signals can disagree. A failed changed-rule rebuild also causes already-rebuilt pages to be discarded on the next attempt. **Proposed target:** one authoritative verified outcome; no incomplete generation eligible for training; an interrupted rebuild resumes verified work and produces the same pages and labels as an uninterrupted run. Evidence: completeness items 20, 23 and 25.
5. **in-scope: comparative proof.** The recorded 291-page “clean” result comes from the same dictionary-based checks that miss the demonstrated cases. There is no independent pixel assessment, entity-recall measurement or head-to-head cue-preservation result. **Proposed target:** compare both pipelines on identical synthetic inputs, reporting per-type precision/recall, surviving details, refusal rate, cue retention, runtime and operator actions. Presidio provides an evaluation framework for this kind of comparison. [Presidio evaluation documentation](https://github.com/data-privacy-stack/presidio/blob/main/docs/evaluation/index.md).
6. **out-of-scope: proof that these pages improve Loupe.** Page counts do not establish that removing visual cues improves detection or false positives. **Target:** a controlled training comparison on the same held-out set, including the South African slice, with no recall or false-positive regression; assess the governing ≥95% detection and ≤2% false-positive targets at product level. Owners: **D03 T04 §13 and 08-eval**. Evidence: [training.md:52](../../glimmer/training.md#L52), [design specification:220](../../specs/2026-10-26-phishing-invoice-analyser-design.md#L220).
